CGPA vs Percentage — What Indian Employers Want

CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) is a grading system used by universities to evaluate a student's overall academic performance across semesters. In India, the 10-point CGPA scale is most common, especially post the UGC's push for grading-based evaluation.

Most Indian competitive exam boards and employers still prefer percentage. When you see "minimum 60% in graduation," you need to convert your CGPA to percentage using your university's official formula to check eligibility.

For UPSC and SSC: There is no minimum percentage requirement for graduation eligibility, so CGPA doesn't need conversion. But for RBI Grade B, CAT (IIM), and some PSU jobs, a percentage cutoff exists — you must convert.

Tip: Always use your university's official multiplier. Don't just multiply by 10 or 9.5 blindly, as it could lead to wrongly claiming eligibility in your application form.

Frequently Asked Questions

How to convert CGPA to percentage for CBSE? +
CBSE formula: Percentage = CGPA × 9.5. So 8.5 CGPA = 8.5 × 9.5 = 80.75%.
What is the Anna University CGPA to percentage formula? +
Anna University formula: Percentage = (CGPA − 0.5) × 10. For 8.0 CGPA: (8.0 − 0.5) × 10 = 75%.
How to convert VTU CGPA to percentage? +
VTU formula: Percentage = (CGPA − 0.75) × 10. For 8.5 CGPA: (8.5 − 0.75) × 10 = 77.5%.
Is 7 CGPA good? +
Yes, 7.0 CGPA is generally good. On the CBSE scale it equals 66.5%, which qualifies for most government jobs and post-graduate programs requiring 60%.
What CGPA is equivalent to 60%? +
Using CBSE formula: 60% ÷ 9.5 ≈ 6.32 CGPA. Using Anna University formula: 60% ÷ 10 + 0.5 = 6.5 CGPA. Varies by university.
Is CGPA or percentage better? +
Both have uses. CGPA is fairer as a holistic measure. Percentage is more universally understood in India by employers and government exam boards. When in doubt, show both on your resume.
Is this CGPA calculator free and accurate? +
Yes, completely free. The formulas used are based on official university notifications. Always cross-check with your university's latest circular as formulas occasionally get updated.
